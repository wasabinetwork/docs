## When Governance or Funding Becomes the Bottleneck

You may be facing one or more of these problems:

- Important decisions depend on informal relationships instead of clear mandates.
- A council, committee, or multisig exists, but its operating rules are incomplete.
- Grants applications arrive faster than reviewers can evaluate them consistently.
- Conflicts of interest, appeals, and exceptions have no credible handling process.
- Community members cannot see why decisions were made or what happened afterward.
- Capital is allocated, but milestones, evidence, and results are not systematically tracked.
- Token-holder votes, committee recommendations, and operational execution do not connect.
- Funders, contributors, beneficiaries, and operators have misaligned expectations.
- The organization needs experienced operating capacity without immediately hiring a full-time governance or grants team.

**The desired outcome:** a system where people know **who decides, by what criteria, using what evidence, with what safeguards, and how decisions translate into action**.

## Ways to Work With GAMA Works

Rather than prescribing a large transformation project, we scope an engagement around the specific decision, funding, or coordination problem.

### 1. Governance & Funding Health Check

**Best for:** teams that have an existing system, but need a clear outside diagnosis before changing it.

We review the current governance, grantmaking, or funding operating model to identify where it works, where it is fragile, and what should change first.

**Possible deliverables**

- Stakeholder and decision-rights map
- Current-state process review
- Governance and operational risk register
- Council, committee, or multisig workflow assessment
- Conflict-of-interest and escalation gap analysis
- Prioritized improvement roadmap
- Findings and decision workshop

**You leave with:** an evidence-informed diagnosis and a practical sequence of changes—not just a generic audit.

### 2. Governance System Design & Implementation

**Best for:** DAOs, foundations, protocols, and organizations formalizing responsibility or improving an existing governance process.

**Possible deliverables**

- Governance operating model and role definitions
- Council, committee, delegate, or working-group structure
- Proposal intake, discussion, review, voting, and execution workflow
- Decision thresholds, approval matrices, and escalation routes
- Multisig signer responsibilities and operational runbooks
- Disclosure and conflict-of-interest procedures
- Public decision records, reporting templates, and operating cadence
- Training, rollout, and first-cycle support

**You leave with:** clearer authority, safer handoffs, and repeatable governance operations.

### 3. Grants Program Build & Operate

**Best for:** foundations, protocols, public-goods programs, and funds launching or improving grants rounds.

A grants program is more than an application form and a payout. It needs eligibility, evaluation, reviewer coordination, defensible allocation decisions, communications, and follow-through.

**Possible deliverables**

- Program objectives, applicant profile, scope, and eligibility rules
- Application workflow and evaluation rubric
- Reviewer selection, onboarding, calibration, and workload plan
- Conflict-of-interest disclosures and recusal process
- Scoring, shortlisting, deliberation, and allocation workflow
- Applicant communication templates and decision records
- Grantee milestone, evidence, and interim-reporting templates
- Retrospective, program reporting, and improvement backlog

Engagements can cover **program design**, **a specific funding round**, or **ongoing grants operations**, depending on the agreed scope.

**You leave with:** a fundable, operable review process and a clearer accountability trail from applications to outcomes.

### 4. Ecosystem & Funding Framework Design

**Best for:** public-goods, ReFi, community-led, and real-world-impact initiatives where money, participation, governance, and measurable outcomes must work together.

**Possible deliverables**

- Stakeholder and ecosystem map
- Funding lifecycle and capital-flow model
- Contributor, funder, operator, and beneficiary incentive map
- Governance-to-funding decision architecture
- Funding mechanism options and trade-off assessment
- Risks involving capture, concentration, misaligned incentives, and dependency
- Impact and evidence-reporting framework
- Pilot design and phased implementation roadmap

**You leave with:** a clearer model for how funding is decided, deployed, measured, and improved.

### 5. Fractional Operations & Strategic Advisory

**Best for:** founders, councils, funding teams, or ecosystem leads who need ongoing decision support and operating capacity without a permanent senior hire.

**Possible work**

- Governance or grants operating cadence
- Council and reviewer coordination
- Agenda, proposal, and decision preparation
- Funding-program quality assurance
- Sensitive decision and conflict-of-interest process support
- Stakeholder alignment and communication
- Strategic reviews, retrospectives, and process iteration

**You get:** experienced operating support with responsibilities, availability, and decision authority defined in the engagement scope.

## What Better Operations Should Make Possible

Success criteria are agreed before implementation. Depending on the engagement, we may measure:

| Operating area | Useful signals |
| --- | --- |
| **Decision clarity** | Documented owners, approval paths, proposal status, and escalation procedures |
| **Governance throughput** | Time from proposal submission to decision and from approval to execution |
| **Review quality** | Reviewer calibration, rubric consistency, disclosure completion, and exception handling |
| **Funding traceability** | Ability to explain eligibility, scoring, allocation decisions, and payment approval |
| **Delivery accountability** | Milestone reporting, evidence coverage, follow-up completion, and identified blockers |
| **Community legitimacy** | Clarity of decisions, accessible records, structured feedback, and transparent appeals |
| **Operational sustainability** | Documentation quality, repeatable handoffs, manageable workload, and reduced dependency on individuals |

These are **measurement options**, not guaranteed improvements. A program may need some, not all, of them.

## From Governance Decisions to Verified Learning

A governance or funding system is strongest when each stage leaves the next stage better informed.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Define the Mandate">
    Agree what the organization is trying to achieve, who is accountable, what authority exists, and which constraints matter.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Design the Decision Process">
    Set roles, criteria, rights, safeguards, communications, and escalation paths before decisions are contested.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Allocate Resources Transparently">
    Structure evaluation, deliberation, approvals, and documentation so decisions can be reviewed and explained.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Support Execution">
    Translate approvals into assignments, milestones, treasury or payment workflows, and practical follow-through.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Collect Evidence and Feedback">
    Combine quantitative indicators with qualitative feedback, grantee updates, stakeholder perspectives, and relevant verification.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review and Improve">
    Run retrospectives, identify where assumptions failed, document lessons, and revise the next cycle.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Check>
  **The objective is not to create more bureaucracy.** It is to create enough structure for decisions, resources, and responsibilities to remain legible as the ecosystem grows.
</Check>

## A Different Kind of Governance Consulting

Traditional advisory work may end at a framework or slide deck. Purely technical implementations may ship tools without clarifying who is accountable for using them.

GAMA Works works at the intersection:

- **Governance:** who is authorized to decide, and how are they held accountable?
- **Operations:** who does the work, with what cadence and escalation?
- **Funding:** how are priorities translated into allocation decisions and controls?
- **Evidence:** what information shows whether resources produced meaningful progress?
- **Learning:** how do observations influence the next decision cycle?

We favor proportional process, explicit human responsibility, accessible documentation, appropriate technical tooling, and verifiable claims where practical.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Can you design a grants program from scratch?" defaultOpen>
    Yes. Depending on scope, we can help define program objectives, eligibility, review rubrics, council or reviewer responsibilities, conflicts-of-interest handling, allocation workflow, reporting, and operational playbooks.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can you operate an existing funding round?">
    Yes. A round-specific engagement can cover application intake, reviewer coordination, process quality assurance, communications, deliberation support, and post-round documentation. Final funding decisions remain with the authorized body unless separately and explicitly delegated.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can you review a DAO's current governance structure?">
    Yes. We can examine roles, proposal flow, quorum or decision processes, multisig operations, governance debt, escalation, and accountability, then recommend an appropriately scoped improvement plan.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do you only work with Web3 organizations?">
    No. These operating problems also exist in nonprofits, philanthropic funds, foundations, cooperatives, and stakeholder networks. We use blockchain infrastructure when it solves a real problem—not as a prerequisite.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can you help with impact measurement and grant reporting?">
    Yes. We can design milestone reporting, evidence requirements, interim updates, qualitative feedback, quantitative indicators, and retrospectives. Formal assurance or independent verification requires a separately qualified provider when applicable.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Can you build the tools needed to run the program?">
    We can scope a related [Automation & Workflow Design](/automation-workflow-design) or [IT Services](/it-services) engagement for dashboards, portals, integrations, and workflow tooling when they are needed.

    The client's authorized governance body, committee, signers, or accountable decision-maker retains those powers unless a separate agreement expressly assigns a limited responsibility with safeguards and approvals.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Do you offer one-off reviews or ongoing support?">
    Both. We can scope a focused diagnosis or framework project, a specific grants-round engagement, or fractional operating/advisory support. Pricing and availability are determined after discovery.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="What should we prepare before the first conversation?">
    Share the organization and stakeholders, the governance or funding process today, relevant public documents, the decision or bottleneck you need to address, desired outcomes, and any known timing or budget constraints. Do not send private keys, passwords, or confidential applicant data through an unsecured channel.
